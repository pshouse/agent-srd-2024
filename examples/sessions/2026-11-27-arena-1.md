## Findings

1. **Opus 5.5 is the strongest party driver so far, but only just.** It leads on composite (69.86 vs 68.85 for
   the heuristic) and ties `greedy` for the most wins. The lead comes from goblin-ambush: 8/10 wins against the
   heuristic's 2/10, by focusing fire and using Vex/Sap mastery well. In crypt-of-ghouls the heuristic is clearly
   better (6/10 vs 3/10), mostly because its revive-and-heal triage is fast and reliable. Ten seeds is a small
   sample: a 1–2 win swing per scenario is within noise.
2. **Sonnet 5.5 ranks below `greedy`.** It ranks 4th on composite. Its legality and tactical scores are both lower than
   Opus, and in wyrmling-lair it played badly (54.7).
3. **Fire immunity is fully understood.** Both models logged 0 wasted immune hits in 60 matches. `greedy` wasted 37.
   Rationales check it every time, e.g. "Fire spells are useless here since everything is fire-immune" and
   "Piercing damage avoids its fire immunity". In the traced wyrmling matches the only damage spells cast were Magic
   Missile (force) and Spirit Guardians, with no fire spells at all.
4. **Nearly all violations are action-economy bookkeeping in wyrmling-lair:** 34 of 36. This is the only
   scenario with both a Nick rogue (Kaelen) and an Extra Attack fighter (Aldric). The typical failures:
   - a second off-hand Light attack after the Nick attack
   - a third longsword swing at `attacks_remaining: 0`
   - Disengage after the Action is spent

   The engine rejects all of them correctly. Retries make it worse: `retry_feedback` only describes the latest
   rejection. Kaelen went from offhand → disengage → the same offhand again, then the turn was force-ended (seed 8,
   round 2). Filed as **#55**.
5. **The models don't spread out against breath weapons.** In `sonnet-wyrmling-7`, the party "held the line" in a
   15-ft cluster, and Aldric moved *into* the group. The wyrmling and both Cinder Hounds then each caught 3–4 PCs
   in a Fire Breath cone, and the party was at 0 HP before round 2. Sonnet's own rationale mentioned keeping
   "out of a single breath-weapon cone", but its movement didn't follow it. Opus spread out better, but still
   lost 7/10 here. Wyrmling-lair is the hardest scenario for every contender: no one won more than 3/10. At 4 PCs
   and 2,600 XP it's working as intended as the hard tier. Still, watch the round-1 triple-breath alpha strike in
   future tuning.
6. **Table talk is on-topic but repetitive.** Callouts name real targets, flag immunities, ask for heals and
   track Sap/Vex debuffs. That's useful coordination:
   - "Both ghasts are sapped now. Dorian, Healing Word on Lyra ASAP. Each hit on her is a crit while she's down!"
   - "Magmin's down! I'm hanging back at range. Next turn I'll hit the wyrmling with cold or force."

   Two weak spots:
   - **Volume.** The model talks on nearly every intent. In `sonnet-goblin-10` there were 22 messages in 22
     intents, and the last eight were variations of "Talia, stay down. I'm finishing Scout B."
   - **Requests that can't be met.** "Aldric, can you get him a potion?" went to a character with no potions;
     Aldric replied "I can't get a potion to anyone right now."

   Coordination scoring already penalises talk without productive turns (see the talk-spam regression test below).
   It doesn't penalise near-duplicate messages from a player who is also productive.
7. **No engine bugs found.** Every traced match audits clean. One thing looked like a bug at first: Ghoul Bites for 17
   against a 1d6+2 + 1d6 stat block. They were correct auto-crits on a Paralyzed PC. The transcript doesn't say
   "critical" for monster hits or narrate the Claw's paralysis save, so it reads like a bug. Filed as **#56**,
   together with a concentration-loss line printed out of order. Creatures ending movement in the same square
   also shows up in these traces; that's already filed as #53.

## Talk-spam regression test

The CTO asked for a regression test: a talk-spam party (talks every turn, only ever ends its turn) must score
about 0.6× `random` or less and win 0 games. This PR adds it as
`TestTalkSpamRegression` in `tests/test_stage3_arena_integrity.py`.

The bound is checked on the **pre-tuning calibration line-ups**: the enemy lists from before #48, pinned in the
test as `_CALIBRATION_ENEMIES`. It isn't checked on the tuned built-in scenarios:

| Line-ups | talk-spam | random | ratio | talk-spam wins |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| calibration, seeds 1–3 (test) | 17.24 | 32.75 | **0.526** | 0 |
| calibration, seeds 1–10 | 16.20 | 35.97 | 0.450 | 0 |
| tuned main, seeds 1–3 | 15.82 | 19.95 | 0.79 | 0 |

On the tuned line-ups, `random` also wins nothing and dies in a turn or two, so both baselines bottom out near the
same floor and the ratio means little. On the calibration line-ups `random` survives long enough to score, and
there the 0.6× bound measures what it's meant to: talking without acting isn't rewarded. The existing check
(talk-spam < `random` and < 0.3× heuristic on the tuned scenarios) stays in place.
